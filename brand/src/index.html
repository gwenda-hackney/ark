01

Brand Overview

Gwenda is a canalside ristobar at Schwartz Wharf, Hackney Wick, London — opening Summer 2026. It is not a restaurant with a drinks list. Not a bar with a kitchen annex. An archive salon where food, drink, art, and sound converge.

Craft cocktails, Italian small plates, natural wines, and vinyl on the decks — refined for candlelight and elevated for the lounge. The brand is deliberately, intentionally mysterious. Who is Gwenda? is a question, not an answer.

Ship palette

Gwenda sits at Schwartz Wharf on the Hackney Wick canal — a converted wharf, not a themed restaurant. The palette evokes a ship's world: blacks, browns, and gold. Hull black and tar in shadow. Teak, aged timber, and warm iron in the mid-tones. Brass hardware and candlelight as accent — timber, iron, and lamplight in a hold or salon below deck.

03

Colour

The palette is warm-dark and intentionally restrained. Every colour has a specific role. Do not introduce new colours.

Colour scheme

Monochromatic

Gold · cream · charcoal

Black, brown, and gold from the ship palette — all hues cluster around ~43° on the wheel. Cream and charcoals share the same hue at different saturation and lightness; gold is the accent. The colours evoke hull shadow, aged timber, and brass in lamplight.

04

Typography

Gwenda's identity is entirely serif. Do not use sans-serif typefaces in branded communications. Cormorant Garamond and Lora evoke ship's-log lettering and engraved brass — editorial, archival, unhurried. Wide letter-spacing on labels (hero location, eyebrows, navigation) mirrors nameplate capitals on a vessel.

Print & off-screen

Canva, business cards, menus, and any flat fill that is not a layered screen render. Use foundation swatches from §03 — especially warm charcoal #23211b.

Print vs screen — the story section

The story section on the website is not a flat fill — it layers a dark base, a 4% gold glow, and 3.5% film grain. For print and Canva, always use the warm charcoal flat equivalent:

Story section — as seen on screen
Print / Canva equivalent

Warm Charcoal #23211b — use this for all flat print and off-screen design work. Do not use #141414 or #151414 for print — they are too dark and too cold.

Film grain (off-screen)

Replicate the on-screen grain in Canva or print by overlaying a noise texture at 3.5% opacity on flat fills — especially warm charcoal #23211b. See §07 Digital for the live site implementation.

Logo on print

Gold wordmark on warm charcoal #23211b or hull black #0d0d0d; black-only variant on cream #e8e4dc for light paper. Full specs — §05 Logo.

Photography

Food, drink, canal, and interior imagery for the site, social, and print. Colour tokens for green grading — §03 Brown-hue greens.

Principles

Green & brown-hue materials

Green does not join gold as a fourth UI accent. The only green that fits the ship palette is brown-hue green — olive, moss, canal mud — so desaturated it reads as timber shadow before it reads as green. Bright cold greens stay in rejected territory.

Place

Canal water, wharf brick, moss on stone — photography only. Schwartz Wharf and the Wick bring natural green; hero treatment (brightness(0.55) + warm gradient) pulls it toward timber brown.

Plate

Herbs, olive oil, natural wine, seasonal produce — content and imagery. Italian small plates and canalside setting imply green without a swatch.

Patina

Oxidized brass and damp timber — shadow tones only. Dark olives and verdigris that read as near-black brown, never brighter than warm charcoal.

Brown-hue greens — timber's shadow

The viable greens are not “green” on the wheel — they sit beside warm brown and charcoal, same family as teak and damp deck. Think moss on timber, mud at the canal edge, olive oil in shadow. Hue roughly 70–95° at very low saturation; lightness never above warm charcoal #23211b.

Brown-hue greens are a continuation of the brown lane — not a separate accent. If you squint, canal shadow should be hard to tell from warm charcoal.

In menu and social photography, grade greens toward olive-brown, not emerald:

✗ Cold green — sage/emerald in the plate. Reads healthy and bright; pulls away from candlelight.
✓ Brown-hue green — same dish, graded toward #3a3828 / #1e261c. Herb reads as food in shadow, not brand colour.

On the wheel

Brown-hue greens hug the timber cluster

Gold and charcoals sit around ~43° (warm). Viable greens are olive-browns at ~70–95° with very low saturation — close enough to read as damp timber, not a second accent. Cold greens at ~120°+ stay off-palette. Verdigris #2a3530 is the cool limit; prefer canal shadow and damp timber for anything in the brown family.

Do not use as accents

Bright greens break the candlelight logic and compete with gold:

✗ Sage · too hospitality
✗ Emerald · too saturated
✗ Mint · too bright

Brown-hue greens extend the brown material lane only. Gold stays the sole UI accent. See also docs/colors.md.
